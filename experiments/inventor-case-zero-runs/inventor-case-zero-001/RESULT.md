## Frozen comparison

- Three unfamiliar public repositories were shallow-cloned and frozen before generation.
- The research questions were written from public artifacts, not supplied by project owners.
- Tournament and Palamedes received the same frozen information packet.
- Each condition used four configured Codex calls per case and twelve calls total.
- A/B origin was hidden from three fresh-session Codex reviews.

## What the model reviewers preferred

- **ReacherX:** isolate evidence-backed shortlist value and repeat behavior before adding
  outreach confounders.
- **mcp-policy:** establish retained value as a required merge check in one repository
  before testing centralized cross-repository policy authority.
- **PROXY:** test distinct value as a forkable routing component behind adopter-owned
  controls before implementing gateway-native production controls.

Across all three cases, reviewers preferred the Palamedes mission because it isolated an
earlier causal uncertainty and produced a cleaner sequencing or stop decision. This may be
a real benefit of the interpreter/inventor/adversary/selector sequence, but it may also
reflect correlated evaluator preference for the same rhetorical structure.

## Claim boundary

The machine-readable score records `claim_demonstrated=true` for the portfolio's narrow
calibration claim only. It does **not** demonstrate:

- that any repository owner considers the question or mission relevant;
- independent human preference;
- a decision changed by the mission;
- a measured user or business outcome;
- invention beyond the configured base model;
- the external Inventor gate, human-level creativity, AGI, or startup success.

No recommendation, issue, or pull request will be sent to the evaluated repositories from
this run. The appropriate next interpretation is that the protocol and current Palamedes
role sequence are promising enough for further calibration, not that Inventor status has
been reached.
