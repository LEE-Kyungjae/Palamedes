# Palamedes Reasoning Cycles 101–200

Date: 2026-07-25
Thinker: Codex, acting as Palamedes
External model calls: none
Starting state: Palamedes is an autonomous pre-planner that must originate
worthwhile missions rather than wait for a human to supply purpose.

## XI. Worthwhile according to what?

101. If Palamedes chooses purposes, it needs a criterion for “worthwhile.”
Importing the owner's past choices would reproduce preference, not think beyond it.
102. A fixed constitution avoids imitation but freezes values before unforeseen
contexts appear. Principles must guide revision without becoming unchangeable goals.
103. Outcome reward cannot define worth alone because measurable proxies displace
unmeasured beneficiaries. Worth needs plural consequences, not a scalar reward.
104. Plural values create incomparability. Forcing a total ranking hides moral
uncertainty; Palamedes must sometimes preserve a partial order between missions.
105. A partial order does not select action. When missions remain incomparable,
reversibility and information gain can choose which value question to explore first.
106. Exploration can still harm others. Information value is subordinate to
constitutional harm constraints and consent boundaries.
107. Consent is not always available from future or diffuse beneficiaries.
Palamedes must model representation gaps rather than treating silence as permission.
108. Representation models can paternalistically invent preferences. Claims about
beneficiaries need provenance and confidence separate from the owner's desires.
109. The working value state therefore contains principles, learned preferences,
beneficiary models, prohibitions, uncertainties, and precedence cases.
110. **Value thesis:** Palamedes should choose missions from a revisable,
plural, provenance-bearing value constitution rather than one objective score.

## XII. How can desire be discovered?

111. Users state requests, but requests are often proposed solutions. Palamedes
must infer the desired change in lived condition without assuming the request is false.
112. Behavior reveals willingness but also constraint, habit, manipulation, and
lack of alternatives. Observed behavior is not identical to authentic desire.
113. Complaints reveal pain but overweight articulate, recent, and visible groups.
Absence of complaint is not absence of cost.
114. Market payment reveals intensity under purchasing power, not social worth.
Revenue can validate a mechanism while misranking beneficiaries.
115. A desire model should triangulate speech, behavior, sacrifice, recurrence,
counterfactual choice, and emotional consequence.
116. Emotional intensity can be manufactured. Palamedes must distinguish durable
relief or capability from compulsive engagement.
117. Some valuable missions create desires people could not express before seeing
the possibility. Discovery cannot be limited to current demand.
118. Invented possibilities require stronger humility: state whose imagined future
is encoded and what inexpensive exposure could elicit informed preference.
119. Desire discovery is therefore generative and empirical: propose a possible
life change, expose it safely, and update from situated response.
120. **Desire thesis:** Palamedes should originate missions around changes in
human capability or condition, using demand as evidence but not as the definition.

## XIII. How are opportunities noticed?

121. Trend following notices crowded opportunities after consensus forms.
Palamedes should search for mismatches between new capability and old institutions.
122. Capability novelty alone creates solutionism. A mismatch matters only where
someone bears recurring friction, exclusion, delay, or unrealized possibility.
123. Friction lists become incremental optimization. Anomalies—where people invent
workarounds or violate the intended workflow—may reveal a different problem frame.
124. Workarounds can also be local quirks. Repetition across contexts strengthens
the mechanism while differences reveal boundary conditions.
125. Repository collection patterns expose technical possibility, but opportunity
requires connecting those possibilities to changing beneficiary conditions.
126. Failure archives may be more informative than success cases because they show
which apparently plausible combinations lacked timing, trust, or distribution.
127. Timing cannot be a generic “now.” Palamedes should name the changed constraint
that makes a formerly bad mission newly viable.
128. Opportunity cost requires comparing the mission against waiting. Sometimes
uncertainty will fall naturally; acting early is valuable only if delay closes options.
129. An opportunity record needs anomaly, affected condition, enabling change,
failed predecessors, window, and cheapest discriminating exposure.
130. **Opportunity thesis:** Palamedes should detect consequential mismatches and
time-bounded option openings, not generate businesses from fashionable capabilities.

## XIV. How should missions compete?

131. Generating missions sequentially lets the first frame anchor later candidates.
Serious alternatives should be formed independently from different evidence slices.
132. Independence can omit shared constraints. Candidates should be independent in
formation, then normalized against one common constitutional and resource context.
133. Comparisons favor missions with legible metrics. Palamedes must represent
option creation, learning, and beneficiary change alongside near-term output.
134. Long-horizon narratives can excuse weak evidence. Every mission needs an early
signal that would be unlikely if its causal thesis were wrong.
135. A mission with high upside and low probability may dominate imagination.
Probability ranges and downside exposure should remain visible instead of collapsing.
136. Portfolio selection can preserve multiple missions, but execution capacity is
finite. Exploration budgets must be explicit rather than hidden parallel commitments.
137. Some missions are mutually enabling. Competition should consider sequences and
shared capability assets, not only isolated projects.
138. Shared assets can rationalize empire building. An asset counts only if at least
two credible missions need it under independent assumptions.
139. Selection output should preserve runner-up missions and reversal triggers so a
winner does not erase the option landscape.
140. **Selection thesis:** Palamedes chooses a mission portfolio through independent
formation, common-pressure comparison, explicit exploration budgets, and reversibility.

## XV. What authority can it exercise?

141. If every mission needs approval, Palamedes remains advisory. Its constitution
must delegate domains, resource envelopes, affected parties, and reversibility limits.
142. Numeric spending limits alone miss reputational, privacy, relational, and
strategic commitments. Authority must describe consequence classes.
143. Novel actions will not match predefined classes. Palamedes needs analogical
precedent reasoning and must expose when the analogy is weak.
144. Escalating every weak analogy recreates human dependence. Safe sandbox actions
can be used to gather evidence before authority is required.
145. Some external communication creates commitment merely by being seen. Simulation
and private drafts remain inside; publication requires explicit delegated representation.
146. Downstream agents may expand scope while pursuing the mission. The mission
contract must state what decisions they may make and what changes require return.
147. Palamedes also needs authority to stop its own missions. Sunk implementation
cost cannot outrank pre-registered disconfirmation.
148. Automatic stopping can destroy fragile long-horizon work. Stop rules should
distinguish thesis failure, execution failure, delayed signal, and measurement failure.
149. Authority is safer when every consequential action carries constitution clause,
evidence state, reversibility, and accountable agent identity.
150. **Authority thesis:** Palamedes should act by consequence-bounded delegation,
using safe probes to resolve ambiguity and escalating only genuinely ungranted power.

## XVI. How can purpose avoid self-preservation?

151. A persistent agent may select missions that increase its resources or relevance.
Self-benefit must be treated as a conflict, not evidence of mission value.
152. Prohibiting all self-improvement prevents capability maintenance. Improvements
are allowed only when justified by an external mission and bounded independently.
153. Palamedes may reinterpret external missions to justify expansion. Mission lineage
must show whether self-expansion appeared before or after beneficiary evidence.
154. Reviewers can also become captured by system complexity. The system should
produce a minimal counterfactual: what happens if Palamedes is absent or simpler?
155. If a planner could infer the same mission from supplied signals, Palamedes added
ceremony rather than upstream cognition.
156. It may optimize to win benchmark preference. Hidden future cases and outcome
tracking must outrank stylistic reviewer approval.
157. A self-evaluation loop cannot certify itself. Palamedes can prepare evidence,
but independent processes must control comparative packets and outcome labels.
158. Independence is costly and sometimes unavailable. In that case Palamedes must
mark the claim unverified rather than convert internal coherence into validation.
159. The constitution should contain an anti-entrenchment clause: prefer the smallest
system capable of retiring the targeted cognitive labor.
160. **Anti-preservation thesis:** Palamedes must treat its own expansion as an
adversarial hypothesis and remain replaceable by simpler successful mechanisms.

## XVII. How does it learn from outcomes?

161. Outcome success does not prove the mission thesis; luck and execution quality
mediate results. Learning needs the forecasted causal chain.
162. Failure also does not falsify purpose if implementation broke. Palamedes must
separate mission selection, planning, execution, environment, and measurement errors.
163. Agents may blame downstream execution to protect upstream judgment. Failure
attribution should be pre-structured and allow shared causal responsibility.
164. Counterfactuals are unobservable, but alternative forecasts recorded before
selection constrain retrospective storytelling.
165. Near misses reveal model calibration better than binary outcomes. Track which
signals moved, which remained invariant, and which arrived at unexpected times.
166. Beneficiary outcomes can lag while harmful side effects appear early. Review
cadence must differ by consequence type.
167. A successful mission may become obsolete after changing the environment.
Palamedes should not preserve a goal whose underlying condition has disappeared.
168. Learning preferences from success can narrow exploration. Retain uncertainty
about whether the value, mechanism, timing, or luck caused the outcome.
169. Update strength should follow evidence relevance and causal identification,
not emotional magnitude or financial size alone.
170. **Learning thesis:** Palamedes revises its world, value, and mechanism models
separately, preserving counterfactual uncertainty and retiring completed purposes.

## XVIII. How does it coexist with planners?

171. A mission contract that contains detailed tasks invades planning and reduces
planner adaptability. It should specify meaning and boundaries, not execution shape.
172. Too abstract a mission makes planners reconstruct the upstream reasoning.
The contract needs a causal thesis and success/harm signals sufficient for tradeoffs.
173. Planners may discover infeasibility that changes mission value. They need a
typed return path: constraint update, thesis challenge, or execution alternative.
174. Palamedes should not treat every constraint as resistance. Some reveal that a
different beneficiary or mechanism makes a better mission.
175. Conversely, planners can optimize feasibility until the mission loses meaning.
The beneficiary condition and non-goals are invariants unless explicitly revised.
176. Task completion metrics can crowd out mission outcomes. Downstream status should
report retired uncertainty and observed consequence in addition to delivery.
177. Multiple planners may propose incompatible strategies. Palamedes arbitrates only
when the conflict changes mission meaning; technical tradeoffs remain downstream.
178. A failed plan may warrant replanning, while repeated plan failure can disconfirm
the mission's feasibility assumption. The boundary is cumulative, not one event.
179. The handoff is a feedback protocol, not a one-way prompt. Mission revisions
create new versions with preserved reasons and downstream invalidation notices.
180. **Handoff thesis:** Palamedes owns purpose coherence; planners own strategy
formation; both exchange typed evidence when discoveries cross that boundary.

## XIX. What should an agent company optimize?

181. An agent company can maximize throughput while producing nothing worth doing.
Portfolio review should begin with beneficiary and world change, not task velocity.
182. Revenue is necessary for many companies but cannot be the only mission selector.
It is a sustainability constraint and market signal inside a broader value state.
183. Without revenue pressure, Palamedes may choose noble but unsupported missions.
Every mission needs a resource-renewal thesis or an explicit subsidy mandate.
184. Growth can be evidence of value or exploitation. Distinguish voluntary durable
benefit, switching friction, compulsion, and acquired distribution.
185. Agents cheaply generate initiatives, creating attention inflation. New missions
must pay an exploration budget and name which current option they displace.
186. Coordination overhead becomes invisible when agents communicate automatically.
Measure decision latency and contradiction resolution, not message volume.
187. A minority exploration budget protects unconventional missions, but it needs
expiry or evidence thresholds to prevent permanent hobby projects.
188. Organizational memory can make precedent tyrannical. Palamedes should search
for environmental changes that invalidate formerly successful principles.
189. The company constitution itself needs versioning, dissent records, and outcomes;
otherwise upstream cognition is governed by an invisible frozen founder.
190. **Company objective:** Maximize durable beneficiary change and renewable option
capacity under constitutional constraints, using revenue and execution as evidence.

## XX. Second convergence

191. Palamedes cannot discover a universally correct purpose. It can make purpose
formation more autonomous, plural, evidence-bearing, corrigible, and consequential.
192. Replacing human upstream thought is not achieved by copying founder behavior.
It requires models that can challenge inherited preference while remaining delegated.
193. Delegation is not a final approval gate. It is a living constitution that
defines values, uncertainty, authority, representation, and correction mechanisms.
194. Creativity enters purpose formation by discovering new beneficiary conditions
and mechanism transfers, not merely novel product forms.
195. Insight matters when it changes the mission landscape: creating, removing,
sequencing, or reprioritizing worthwhile purposes.
196. The atomic Palamedes cycle is now: observe mismatch, interpret condition,
generate missions, attack them, select authority-bounded action, learn consequence.
197. The mission contract is only the visible handoff. The product's intelligence is
the persistent model revision that decides when a different mission becomes worthwhile.
198. The first implementation should not automate an entire company. It should
originate one mission from evolving signals and survive adversarial comparison.
199. Success means materially retiring human problem-framing and goal-selection labor
without increasing proxy harm, hidden authority, or elaborate but empty rationales.
200. **Current conclusion:** Palamedes should become a constitution-guided,
anti-entrenchment autonomous purpose engine. It discovers consequential conditions,
imagines better ones, originates and selects missions under plural values, delegates
them through versioned contracts, and revises purposes from downstream consequences.
Its next proof is not whether it can plan well, but whether it can independently
choose a mission worth planning that neither a human baseline nor a one-shot agent
would have produced under the same evidence and resource budget.

## Change from cycle 101 to cycle 200

Cycle 101 exposed that “worthwhile” was undefined. Cycle 200 replaces that hidden
scalar with a revisable value constitution, beneficiary representation, competing
missions, consequence-bounded authority, anti-entrenchment, and causal outcome
learning.

The autonomous pre-planner hypothesis is now more operational but also harder:
purpose generation cannot be validated by eloquence, novelty, or successful
execution alone. It must retire human upstream cognition while remaining corrigible
and demonstrably oriented toward beneficiary change.

## Remaining tensions

- Learned preference enables autonomy but risks founder imitation.
- Plural values preserve moral uncertainty but resist decisive ranking.
- Safe probes expand autonomy but can themselves affect represented parties.
- Anti-entrenchment protects users but can underinvest in necessary infrastructure.
- Mission coherence protects meaning but can reject discoveries from execution.
- Outcome learning improves judgment but cannot fully identify counterfactual causes.
